# The hero model (H10)

`build.py` turns the Quaternius **Universal Animation Library 1 + 2** (the clips and the rig) and **Universal Base
Characters** (the body; the designer chose its male Superhero body over the UAL mannequin), all CC0, "Standard" edition,
into `assets/models/hero.glb`: the H10 hero (`art/generated/H10-hero-hood.jpg`, DESIGN 14) with only the clips the game uses.
Everything on top of the Quaternius rig is generated by the script, so the model can be rebuilt and tweaked.

## Run

```sh
# the packs, unzipped anywhere (the script searches the folder for UAL1_Standard.glb, UAL2_Standard.glb and
# Superhero_Male_FullBody.gltf)
blender -b --python tools/hero/build.py -- --src ~/Downloads/quaternius
# options
#   --out <file.glb>     default assets/models/hero.glb
#   --preview <dir>      also renders Cycles stills (turntable, close-ups, a few poses), a few minutes on a CPU
#   --blend <file>       saves the built scene for a look in Blender
#   --body <name>        ubc-male (default) | ubc-female | mannequin. ubc-*: a Quaternius "Universal Base Characters"
#                        body (CC0, the same 65-bone rig; Superhero_*_FullBody.gltf searched for under --src). Its mesh
#                        and weights are used as they are; collar, hood, visor, hips and coat bones follow its proportions
#                        (fit_to_body). mannequin: the UAL's own body, voxel-remeshed and smoothed into one suit.
```

Blender 4.5 (tested with 4.5.3 LTS). ~10 s without previews. The output is deterministic (seeded random).
Use the in-place `UAL*_Standard.glb` files, not the `_RM` (root motion) ones.

## What the glb contains

About 36k triangles, 3.0 MB (no Draco, no textures, no UVs; the 19 clips are about half of it), for the default
`ubc-male`; the `mannequin` build is about 47k triangles, 3.4 MB.

| Node / material | What | Used by `view/hero.ts` for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Body` (`Suit`, `SuitJoints`) | `ubc-male`: the Superhero_Male mesh as it is (one `Suit` material). `mannequin`: the mannequin voxel-remeshed (6 mm) into one closed suit, smoothed (hands left alone), decimated to 20k triangles; weights carried over from the mannequin and blurred so joints bend softly; `SuitJoints` where the mannequin had its joint pieces | matte black suit, fresnel rim |
| `Armor` (`Armor`) | plates cut out of the suit along clean outlines (shoulders, forearms, knees, shins, outer thighs), lifted 4.5 mm and 4.5 mm thick | glossier plates, fresnel rim |
| `SuitLines` (`NeonLines`) | ribbons traced from outside (they ride over the plates): outer leg to shin front, top-back of the arms, two chest lines | the hero color (ending counter) |
| `Coat` (`Coat`, `NeonLines`, `Trim`) | sleeveless long coat, 104+ columns, open at the front, a stand-up collar that is high at the back and slopes to the front, two back seams in `NeonLines`, a thin `Trim` along the collar top and the front edges; below 0.8 m it splits into tapering strips. Float attribute `_glow` (0 -> 1 toward the strip tips) | fresnel rim, emissive glow of the strip tips, dim trim |
| `Filaments` (`Filaments`) | 4 thin ribbons hanging from each strip; `_glow` 1 at the root -> 0 at the tip | additive fade + sway shader |
| `Hood` (`Coat`, `Trim`), `Visor` (`Visor`) | hood skinned to head / neck / chest; its lower edge narrows and ends inside the collar (no step); the solidify rim around the face opening is `Trim`; the glowing visor strip inside | hood material (stronger rim), visor color |
| `Wrist` (`Gun`, `Holo`, `NeonLines`) | band and hologram panel on the left forearm | flickers while hacking |
| `Gunblade` empty (child of `hand_r`) | sword grip, scale 1.25; children `GunBody` (`Gun`, `NeonLines`), `GunCore` (`Core`: a tube in the top channel and a lit cell on the back), `Blade` (`Blade`), `Muzzle` empty | blade shown in sword mode, retracted in rifle mode; core brighter in rifle mode, flares on shots |
| `RifleHold` empty (child of `hand_r`) | where `Gunblade` sits in rifle mode, scale 1.75 | the view blends position, rotation and scale |
| bones `coat_{l,r}{1..6}_{a,b,c,d}` | 12 three-bone chains around the hips (parent `pelvis`), joints at 0.97 / 0.74 / 0.53 / 0.31 m; `d` is a non-deforming leaf that marks where `c` points | swung at runtime: springs, trail, crouch lift, lie along the body when dead, pushed out of the legs, kept off the floor |

| `jump_start`, `jump_loop`, `jump_land` | Jump_Start, Jump_Loop, Jump_Land (1) | first 0.3 s in the air, then the loop; land after > 0.35 s in the air |

### Foot sync

The clips' own speeds (planted foot, measured on the `ubc-male` legs, which are 4-7 % longer than the mannequin's):
walk 1.02 m/s (1.36 m per 1.33 s cycle), jog 5.91 m/s (5.50 m per 0.93 s), sprint 9.25 m/s (6.20 m per 0.67 s),
crouch walk 0.74 m/s (1.48 m per 2.0 s). (Mannequin: 1.31, 5.35, 5.91 and 1.43 m per cycle.) A blend's stride is not the
average of the clips' strides, so `view/hero.ts` keeps a table of the walk -> run blend's measured stride (`WR_STRIDE`,
every 10 % of run share; run -> sprint is close to linear). Each frame it picks the run share whose stride gives about
1.15 cycles/s at the current speed, fades sprint in from 4.3 to 5.8 m/s, and advances one shared phase by
`speed / stride of the current weights`. Checked headless with three.js (on the mannequin): the planted foot moves at the hero's speed
(within 1 %) from 1.2 to 7 m/s, so the walk / sprint speeds in `config.json` can change without retuning.
Re-measure the table if the clips or the body change: pose the blend with three.js (AnimationMixer, run and sprint
shifted by `RUN_PHASE`), sample `ball_l` / `ball_r` over 200 steps of one cycle and take the median speed of the foot
that is within 2.5 cm of the floor, per run share 0, 0.1 .. 1. Sprint: run -> sprint at 1.0; crouch: crouch_walk alone.

## Changing things

- Shapes, sizes, line paths, hem length and strip count: the constants and functions in `build.py` (`BODY_*`, `PLATES`,
  `GAP`, `Z_CUT`, `COLS`, `STRIP_COLS`, `FLARE`, `collar_top()`, `collar_r()`, `LINE_THETAS`, `CHAINS`, `CHAIN_Z`,
  `build_hood()`, `build_gunblade()`, `GUN_SCALE`, `RIFLE_SCALE`...).
- A different clip: edit `CLIPS` (name in the glb, source name, pack). A new state also needs code in `view/hero.ts`.
- Colors, rim strength and glow are set in `view/hero.ts` (`RIM_*`, `TRIM_K`, `LINES_K`, `FILAMENT_K`, ...; the glb
  materials are replaced by name), not in Blender. The trim and the rim stay below the bloom threshold on purpose.
